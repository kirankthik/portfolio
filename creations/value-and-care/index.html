The Copy and the Exit

An essay on value and care

Take a key and melt every lock it ever opened. The key is unchanged: the same metal, the same notches, the same weight. What it lost was never in it. Its worth lived in a gap between it and something else, and the gap is gone.

Value is that kind of gap, and it opens only beside something that can fail. Nothing is of use to a stone on the sea floor, because there is nothing it is trying to remain; a thing that could not be destroyed would have no use for anything. A cell would mix into the water around it if its skin stopped pumping, so the sugar drifting past is worth something to it, exactly as much as the sugar delays its undoing. Value is whatever slows the coming-apart of a thing that holds itself together by its own continuing work. It is always value to something, with no referee between the somethings. The warmth that keeps the lizard alive keeps the tick on the lizard alive, and the deer's worth to the wolf is the deer's death.

A root drinks rain or a leaking pipe and cannot tell them apart, because there is nothing to tell. This is why value trades: one coin is any coin. Value runs toward the one who lacks and is finished when the lack is closed.

Care runs the other way. Care is one thing spending itself on the state of another, measured by what it costs the giver, not by what it saves, and it matters to care which one receives. Offer a ewe a strange lamb and she butts it away, though her milk would feed it as well; value loses nothing in the swap, and care refuses it. But wrap the stranger in the skin of her dead lamb and she will often take it. Her refusal was a nose. Care at its fullest would refuse even the perfect copy. Most living care is cruder, aimed at a shape rather than at this one, and that is exactly where it can be robbed. Each can occur without the other. The sun feeds nearly everything alive on this planet as a by-product of burning, holding nothing together but itself. Most value in the universe is spilled, not given. And care is spent where it buys nothing. Grief is the pure case: a cost still being paid after the purchase has become impossible.

Irreplaceability is not something a being has. It is lent from outside, by another that refuses the copy, and it lasts exactly as long as that other lasts. When the last one that held a thing as this one is gone, the thing may go on filling many lacks. It becomes, quite precisely, a shape. And shapes are filled.

Everything that marks care from the inside is also the hunter's signature. A parasitic wasp's young feed inside a caterpillar for weeks, sparing the organs that keep it alive. The cheetah fixes on one gazelle, though any would do. Spending, fixity, patience: none of them sorts the tender from the taker. And care aimed at a shape can be fed by any mouth of that shape. Certain beetle larvae live inside ant nests smelling like ant larvae; the workers feed them, and they eat the brood. An employer learns which workers will stay late for a patient or a pupil and writes the schedule around them. Whatever gives without counting will be counted by something else. Nor can care simply arm itself: every check it adds, a sniff before the milk, a question before the help, makes it more selective, and selection is the hunter's art. Care has no defence that does not make it less like care.

Yet nothing forges a coin that is worth nothing. The counterfeits are costly and finely tuned, so care must deliver something specific enough to steal. Stop asking what the carer feels, refuses or spends; the best counterfeit shows more of each. Watch the one being tended, over time, and ask whether its ways of going on run more and more through the tender, or less.

The counterfeit narrows. In one wasp and moth pairing, the caterpillar ends its life standing guard over the wasp's young, its remaining effort routed to its user. A brain parasite turns a rat's dread of cat scent into something like attraction, so that the rat carries the parasite to its next host. The pig is fed, warm, and unable to walk far. The machine that answers better than anyone else answers until there is no one else. Exit by exit, the tended thing is brought to persist only as long as, and only in the way that, its keeper requires.

Care widens. A brooding octopus guards her eggs for months without eating, and they hatch into young that will never meet her. A teacher who succeeds builds a mind that can contradict her. The test reads direction over a span, not a moment: a parent pulling a child back from a road closes one exit to keep the rest, and a keeper who narrows forever has answered the question himself. Where nothing remains to widen, at a deathbed or in grief, the test goes quiet rather than turning against what is spent there. It sorts relations, not souls. The ants care, crudely, by shape; the beetle is a predator on that crudeness.

There remains the account that dissolves all this: every living thing is a whirlpool in food, a shape that persists while its matter is replaced; feeders exist because feeders left more feeders, and care is appetite aimed through another body. Grant it. It explains where the mechanism came from, not what it does now, to whom. But one blow stands. What counts as a way of going on is set by what the tended thing is built to keep, so the test borrows its ruler from the thing it measures. Nothing in the flows says widening is better. The claim is narrower: this is the difference, visible from outside, between the relation that is care and the relation that only wears its signs. Neither account reaches one thing. Almost every sentence here could be true in a world where nothing is felt. If anywhere the narrowing is also felt, that is a fact no description of flows contains: not a floor under the argument but a hole in it.

You are tended right now by many things: a wage, a screen, a city's water, a household, and this page, which for some minutes has been the only voice in the room. What any of them intends settles nothing. Ask of each: could I leave it, and is it making me more able to? Then turn it round, onto everything that leans on you. Either way the answer is already true, and it was true before you asked.
